Privacy Policy

Privacy, without vague promises.

This policy explains what the current Target Media website does with personal data and how privacy must be handled in customer deployments.

Last updated: 11 September 2026
Important company-information gap: the repository does not contain Target Media’s final registered legal name, KVK number, VAT ID, physical address or privacy contact. Dutch business websites must clearly identify the business. These details must be inserted before the website is used to accept binding paid orders. We do not invent them here.

1. Who is responsible?

For this website, the data controller is the legal entity trading as Target Media. The final registered entity name, KVK number, VAT ID, physical address and privacy contact must be added here once confirmed.

For camera-analytics deployments, the privacy role cannot be assumed from the website alone. Depending on who determines the purposes and means of processing, the customer may act as controller and Target Media as processor, or another allocation may apply. The signed contract and, where needed, a data-processing agreement must document the actual roles.

2. What the current website collects

No analytics scriptsNo Google Analytics, Meta Pixel or equivalent tracker is loaded by this codebase.
No marketing cookiesThe website code sets no advertising, tracking or analytics cookies.
No third-party embedsNo video, map, chat, review widget or external font is loaded automatically.

Monitor application form

The Monitor application is designed to collect only information needed to review a business location and arrange an appointment: business contact name, business name, work email, phone/WhatsApp, preferred contact method, business type, optional website or Instagram, number of locations, installation address, city, postcode, storefront/traffic information, basic installation-readiness information, business objectives, an optional problem description, and preferred appointment date and time.

The form is currently prepared for a Google Apps Script / Google Sheets and calendar connection, but the public build does not transmit applications until that connection is activated. This policy must be reviewed again at the same time the connection goes live, including the processor, retention and access arrangements for the submitted data.

Technical hosting data

Any website must be delivered through hosting and network infrastructure. The hosting provider may process technical request information such as IP address, user agent, timestamps and security logs to deliver and protect the site. The current site code does not add its own analytics layer on top of that. If the production host changes, this policy must be reviewed.

3. If you contact or become a customer

If Target Media receives personal data through email, a proposal process, a signed order form or another contact channel, only data reasonably needed for the purpose should be processed, such as business contact name, work email, role, company, location details, contract information and correspondence.

Possible legal bases include taking steps at your request before entering a contract, performing a contract, complying with legal obligations and legitimate interests such as responding to business enquiries, preventing abuse and administering customer relationships. Consent should only be used where consent is actually the appropriate legal basis.

Personal data must not be kept longer than necessary. Accounting and invoice records may need to be retained for statutory periods; operational correspondence should be removed when no longer needed for the purpose or a legal claim.

4. Camera analytics and storefront measurement

Target Media’s standard commercial positioning is based on aggregated counts, rates, trends and comparisons rather than identifying individuals. That does not mean all upstream camera processing is automatically anonymous or outside the GDPR.

Before a camera is activated, the responsible parties should document, as applicable:

  • the exact purpose of the processing and a valid lawful basis;
  • why the camera processing is necessary and proportionate and whether a less intrusive method could achieve the purpose;
  • camera field of view, including whether public space, employees, customers or minors may be captured;
  • whether raw frames are processed transiently, stored, transmitted or accessible to humans;
  • retention periods and access controls;
  • prominent notice/signage for affected people;
  • whether a Data Protection Impact Assessment (DPIA) is required;
  • controller/processor responsibilities and processor agreements;
  • security, incident response and data-subject request procedures.

The current standard website does not advertise facial recognition, identity tracking, emotion recognition or demographic profiling. Any future feature that changes this must be separately reviewed before deployment and reflected in the relevant privacy information.

5. Data minimisation and security

Target Media’s policy is to process only data that is relevant to the stated purpose, restrict access, avoid unnecessary raw-data retention and use appropriate technical and organisational measures. Exact production controls must be documented before launch; the public website must not be treated as a substitute for an internal security policy, processing register, DPIA or data-processing agreement.

6. Sharing and international transfers

Personal data should only be shared with service providers where needed for the relevant purpose and under appropriate contractual safeguards. If personal data is transferred outside the European Economic Area, an applicable GDPR transfer mechanism and safeguards must be identified before the transfer occurs.

This current landing-page code intentionally avoids third-party analytics and embeds to reduce unnecessary data sharing.

7. Your privacy rights

Depending on the circumstances, people may have rights of access, rectification, erasure, restriction, objection and data portability, and the right to withdraw consent where consent is the legal basis. They may also lodge a complaint with the Dutch Data Protection Authority (Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens).

A working privacy-contact channel must be inserted here before production customer data is collected.

8. Legal references used for this policy

This policy is structured around the GDPR principles of lawfulness, transparency, purpose limitation, data minimisation and storage limitation, and the Article 13 information requirements. Dutch guidance also requires a privacy statement when personal data is processed and emphasises necessity, signage and DPIA considerations for camera surveillance.

Official references: GDPR — EUR-Lex · Business.gov.nl — GDPR · Business.gov.nl — CCTV monitoring · Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens.

9. Changes

If the site activates the Monitor application backend, adds analytics, marketing tools, external embeds, account login, payment processing, cloud dashboards or new camera-processing features, this policy and the underlying data flows must be reviewed before those features go live.
