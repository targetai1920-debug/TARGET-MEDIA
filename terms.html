Clear commercial terms for a B2B service.

These website terms explain the commercial framework Target Media intends to use for business customers. The signed proposal, order form and any service-specific agreement control the actual engagement.

1. Scope and B2B status

Target Media’s services are presented for business customers. This website is informational and does not itself create a binding service contract, process payment or accept an online order. A contract arises only when the parties accept a written proposal, order form or other agreement that identifies the contracting entity and the scope.

If Target Media later sells directly to consumers online, these terms must be reviewed for mandatory consumer-law requirements before that sales flow goes live.

2. Plans and pricing

The public standard-plan prices are:

  • Monitor — €299 per location per quarter;
  • Intelligence — €599 per location per quarter;
  • Growth Partner — custom quarterly pricing after assessment.

Installation, initial configuration, hardware and other project-specific costs are separate unless the written quote says otherwise. VAT treatment, payment timing and any recurring or one-off charges must be shown in the quote or order form before commitment.

The website does not imply automatic renewal. Renewal, notice periods and payment due dates are governed by the signed order form.

3. What the service provides

The service may provide storefront traffic, attention, stopping, timing, historical and contextual metrics depending on the agreed plan and technical setup. Intelligence outputs can include comparisons, alerts, diagnostic observations and recommendations.

These metrics are decision-support estimates. Measurement quality can be affected by camera placement, lighting, reflections, weather, occlusion, crowding, technical downtime, connectivity, calibration and model error. Target Media does not guarantee that every passer-by or event is detected or classified correctly.

Recommendations and possible-cause analysis are not guarantees of causation, revenue uplift or commercial outcome.

4. Illustrations and claims

Dashboard values, charts, percentages and comparison figures shown on the public website are illustrative sample data unless explicitly labelled otherwise. They are not testimonials, verified customer results, market benchmarks or performance guarantees.

The website currently contains no customer-review section. If reviews or case studies are added later, they must be genuine, verifiable and accurately described.

5. Customer responsibilities

The customer is responsible for providing accurate site information, granting agreed access for installation and maintenance, and ensuring that the deployment can be lawfully operated at its location. This can include assessing camera field of view, public-space capture, employee consultation, signage, privacy notices, lawful basis and other local requirements.

Target Media and the customer must agree who acts as controller, processor or other privacy role and enter into any required data-processing agreement before relevant personal-data processing starts.

6. Privacy and prohibited uses

The standard plans are positioned around aggregated business metrics. The public offer does not include facial recognition, identity tracking, emotion recognition or demographic profiling. A customer may not use Target Media services to unlawfully identify, discriminate against, monitor or make consequential decisions about individuals.

Camera processing can still fall within the GDPR before aggregation. A DPIA or other privacy assessment may be required depending on the deployment.

7. Access, availability and changes

Dashboard access, reporting frequency, support level, maintenance windows and any service-level commitment must be stated in the signed service agreement. Unless a specific SLA is agreed, the public website should not be interpreted as promising uninterrupted availability.

Target Media may improve the website and service over time, but material changes to a contracted paid scope should follow the signed agreement.

Unless otherwise agreed in writing, Target Media retains rights in its software, models, dashboards, documentation, branding and service methods. The customer retains its rights in its own business data and materials.

The public site uses code-generated graphics and the Target Media logo rather than stock photography. Target Media must confirm that it owns or is licensed to use the logo and any future media before publication.

9. Confidentiality and data

Confidential information exchanged during a pilot or customer engagement should be protected and used only for the agreed purpose. Data access, subprocessors, retention, deletion and security obligations should be covered in the service agreement and, where required, a data-processing agreement.

10. Payment, cancellation and refunds

Payment terms are set in the written quote or order form. The commercial refund and cancellation framework is described in the Refund Policy. Nothing in these website terms limits rights or remedies that cannot lawfully be excluded.

11. Liability and force majeure

Any limitation of liability must be stated in the signed agreement and interpreted under applicable law. The website does not attempt to exclude liability that cannot legally be excluded.

Events outside reasonable control, such as infrastructure failure, supplier outage or force majeure, may affect performance. The signed agreement should define the consequences where this is commercially relevant.

12. Governing law and disputes

Unless the signed agreement states otherwise, Target Media intends its B2B agreements to be governed by the laws of the Netherlands. The final contracting entity and competent court or dispute mechanism must be identified in the executed agreement.

Customers should first raise complaints directly with Target Media through the formal contact channel included in their proposal or agreement.

13. Website information obligations

Dutch guidance requires business websites to clearly identify the business, including registered/trade name, physical address, contact details and, when services are offered online, KVK and VAT identification details. These fields remain a launch blocker until the founder supplies the real information.
